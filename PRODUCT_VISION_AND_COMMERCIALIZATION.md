# CTOD Product Vision & Commercialization Plan

## Product Identity

**CTOD — Company Talent & Organizational Development**

**Tagline:** BUILDING PEOPLE. DRIVING PERFORMANCE.

CTOD is not merely an employee review system. It is an organizational talent operating system that connects employee identity, coaching, performance reviews, development, career direction, promotion readiness, succession planning, workforce intelligence, and executive decision support.

The review is the structured data-collection engine. The larger value is the organizational intelligence created from that data.

---

## Vision

**Build organizations where every employee has a visible path, every leader understands the strength of their bench, and every talent decision is supported by real organizational data.**

## Mission

**CTOD turns everyday management, coaching, employee development, performance reviews, and career conversations into a living organizational intelligence system that helps companies develop people, identify leaders, protect institutional knowledge, strengthen succession, and improve performance.**

---

## Core Product Story

CTOD connects the full employee-development lifecycle:

**Employee → Coaching → Review → Development → Career Path → Readiness → Succession → Leadership Decisions → Organizational Performance**

The system should allow leadership to answer questions such as:

- Who are our strongest future leaders?
- Who could become a General Manager or other critical role?
- Which locations have weak leadership benches?
- What happens if a manager leaves tomorrow?
- Who wants to advance?
- Who wants to remain a technical or specialist contributor?
- Where are performance problems recurring?
- Are managers actually developing employees?
- Which employees are being overlooked?
- Which reviews are overdue?
- What positions are hardest to develop internally?
- What competencies are weak across the organization?
- Who is ready to transfer?
- What does the bench look like one, two, and three years from now?

---

## CTOD Product Pillars

### PERFORM
Reviews, competencies, goals, accountability, structured evaluation, and performance records.

### DEVELOP
Coaching, development plans, skill building, follow-up, and manager-led employee development.

### GROW
Career direction, advancement paths, specialist paths, employee aspirations, and long-term company goals.

### IDENTIFY
Promotion readiness, emerging talent, high-potential employees, and future leaders.

### PLAN
Succession, depth charts, role coverage, workforce planning, transfer visibility, and bench strength.

### UNDERSTAND
Enterprise analytics, organizational health, trends, risks, talent pipelines, location comparisons, and executive intelligence.

Every future feature should map clearly to one or more of these pillars.

# Commercial Tire Strategy — Industry 001

Commercial Tire is the first full implementation and should be treated as **Industry 001**.

Commercial Tire-specific elements include:

- Job roles
- Review questions
- Competencies
- Location structure
- Market / area structure
- Role terminology
- Promotion paths
- Succession expectations
- Company-specific analytics

The underlying CTOD engine should remain reusable so future customers can become Industry 002, Industry 003, and beyond by supplying their own job roles, competencies, questions, organization structure, and terminology.

---

## Commercial Tire Positioning

Do not pitch CTOD as “better employee reviews.”

The executive positioning should be:

> **CTOD gives Commercial Tire visibility into its entire workforce and leadership pipeline, from an individual employee at a single location all the way to enterprise-level succession and organizational health.**

The review process is only one input into a much larger system.

# Commercial Tire Rollout Plan

## Phase 1 — LOC 040 Live Proof

LOC 040 is the proof environment and first real-world case study.

Use:

- Real employees
- Real six-digit employee numbers
- Real roles
- Real review dates
- Real coaching moments
- Real career conversations
- Real promotion-readiness decisions
- Real goals

Measure:

- Review completion time
- Manager administrative time
- Employee career interests discovered
- Employees identified as Ready Now
- Employees identified as Ready in 1 Year / 2–3 Years
- Specialist-path employees identified
- Development gaps identified
- Coaching captured
- Review completion rate
- Data that leadership did not previously have

The goal is to produce a factual LOC 040 case study showing what CTOD revealed and how managers used it.

## Phase 2 — Executive Demonstration

Present CTOD to Commercial Tire senior leadership using both:

1. **CTOD Vision Film** — emotional and strategic story
2. **CTOD Pitch Mode** — live guided proof inside the application

The executive meeting should show that CTOD is already functioning and that the larger vision is achievable.

## Phase 3 — 90-Day Enterprise Pilot

Propose a controlled pilot instead of requesting immediate company-wide deployment.

Recommended pilot:

- 5–10 locations
- Mix of markets / areas / operating environments
- Real managers
- Real employee rosters
- One complete review cycle
- Active coaching usage
- Talent pipeline collection
- Location health measurement

At the end, CTOD generates an **Enterprise Talent & Organizational Health Report** based on Commercial Tire’s own data.

## Phase 4 — Company-Wide Deployment

After successful pilot validation:

- Configure all Commercial Tire locations
- Confirm all role templates
- Train managers
- Establish executive / area / market permission levels
- Establish review-cycle governance
- Establish coaching expectations
- Establish succession and promotion-readiness definitions
- Deploy enterprise reporting
- Establish long-term support and data governance

# CTOD Pitch Mode

Pitch Mode should be a first-class product feature in the Master application.

A user with appropriate access should click **PITCH MODE** and CTOD should guide executives through a polished start-to-finish story.

## Chapter 1 — The Problem

Opening message:

> **Most organizations know their financial position better than they know their people.**

Show the fragmented reality:

- Reviews in spreadsheets
- Coaching in notebooks
- Career goals in conversations
- Succession plans in managers’ heads
- Performance issues in email
- Potential leaders known locally but invisible corporately

Then introduce:

> **CTOD connects it.**

## Chapter 2 — One Employee

Follow a fictional employee through the system.

Example:

**Employee #123456**

Show:

- Added to the organization
- Six-digit identity becomes permanent record key
- Employee profile
- Coaching
- Review
- Career direction
- Specialist or advancement path
- Goals
- Promotion readiness
- History preserved through transfer

Executive takeaway:

**Every employee creates structured organizational intelligence over time.**

## Chapter 3 — One Manager

Show the Manager workspace.

Key message:

> **CTOD does not create more administrative work. It turns work managers should already be doing into usable organizational data.**

Demonstrate:

- Fast coaching workflow
- Structured categories and reasons
- Minimal manual typing
- Review scheduling
- Job-specific review questions
- Career-direction capture
- Promotion readiness
- Goal setting
- Two-cycle corrective-resolution rule
- Finalization

## Chapter 4 — One Location

Zoom out from employee to location.

Show:

- Review completion
- Employee development
- Coaching trends
- Promotion bench
- Career interests
- Succession depth
- Risk
- Location health

Message:

> **One manager sees the team. Leadership sees the health of the location.**

## Chapter 5 — The Organization

Open Master as the executive command center.

Show:

- Company location map
- Enterprise KPI strip
- Organizational gauges
- Ready Now bench gauge
- Company Talent Pipeline
- Promotion readiness
- Location health
- Trends
- Risk flags
- Goals
- Succession
- Senior Leadership Summary

Core message:

> **Every one of these gauges started with a manager having a better conversation with an employee.**

## Chapter 6 — What the Data Becomes

Demonstrate the long-term strategic value of historical organizational data.

Example:

### Assistant Manager → General Manager Pipeline

- 48 employees want the role
- 19 are 2–3 years away
- 11 are one year away
- 7 are Ready Now
- Idaho expects 3 GM openings in the next 24 months
- Washington expects 4

CTOD can evolve toward identifying a **human-capital supply chain** and eventually generate intelligent warnings such as:

> **Succession Risk: Boise Market**
>
> Three GM positions have no Ready Now successor.
>
> Five Assistant Managers are within one year.
>
> Recommended leadership-development focus: Financial Management and Employee Accountability.
